Fractions and Percentages: A Calm Guide to Everyday Math

By the Calculate Clearly Editorial Team · Educational guide · Last reviewed October 2026

Fractions and percentages frighten people out of proportion to their difficulty, mostly because school taught procedures before meaning. Here is the meaning first: a fraction, a decimal, and a percentage are three ways of writing the same thing — a part of a whole.

One idea, three costumes

“Percent” literally means “per hundred”. 25% is 25 per 100, which is 25/100, which simplifies to 1/4. Once that clicks, conversions stop being memorisation.

Adding fractions without tears

You can only add pieces of the same size, so fractions need a common denominator. For 1/3 + 1/4: rewrite both as twelfths — 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. Multiplying fractions is easier: multiply tops, multiply bottoms. 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5. Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its flip: 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4/1 = 2 — which makes sense: there are two quarters in a half.

The three percentage questions in daily life

“What is X% of Y?” — discounts and tips

20% of $45: 10% is $4.50, so 20% is $9. A 20% tip on $45 is $9; a 20% discount makes the price $36.

“X is what percent of Y?” — scores and progress

Divide, then multiply by 100: 17 correct out of 20 is 17 ÷ 20 = 0.85 = 85%.

“It changed from A to B — by what percent?”

(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100. A price rising from $80 to $92 changed by 12 ÷ 80 = 15% increase. Dividing by the old value is the step people most often get wrong.

Recipe scaling, the stealth fraction test

Doubling a recipe that calls for 3/4 cup means 3/4 × 2 = 6/4 = 1½ cups. Halving 1/3 cup gives 1/6 cup — or, practically, half of a third-cup measure, which is why many cooks keep a tablespoon conversion handy (1/3 cup = 5 tbsp + 1 tsp; half is about 2 tbsp + 2 tsp).

Key takeaway

Convert to whichever costume makes the problem easy — usually percentages for money, fractions for cooking, decimals for everything else. They are the same number underneath.
