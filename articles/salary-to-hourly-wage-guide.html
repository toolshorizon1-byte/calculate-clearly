Salary to Hourly Wage: How Pay Conversions Really Work

By the Calculate Clearly Editorial Team · Educational guide · Last reviewed October 2026

Job ads mix annual salaries and hourly rates, which makes offers hard to compare. The conversion itself is one division — the traps are in the assumptions hiding underneath it.

The basic conversion

A full-time year is conventionally 40 hours a week × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours.

Hourly rate = Annual salary ÷ 2,080

Annual salaryApprox. hourlyApprox. monthly
$40,000$19.23$3,333
$55,000$26.44$4,583
$75,000$36.06$6,250
$100,000$48.08$8,333

Going the other way, multiply an hourly rate by 2,080: $22 an hour ≈ $45,760 a year — before any of the adjustments below.

Adjustment 1: Unpaid time off

The 2,080 figure assumes every week is paid. A contractor at $30/hour who takes three unpaid weeks and has a slow unpaid week at Christmas actually works about 1,920 paid hours: $57,600, not $62,400. Salaried staff with paid leave keep the full amount — one reason a lower-looking salary can be worth more than a higher-looking hourly rate.

Adjustment 2: Overtime and real hours

If a salaried role routinely expects 50-hour weeks, the honest hourly equivalent of $75,000 is not $36 — it is $75,000 ÷ 2,600 ≈ $28.85. Ask about typical hours before comparing offers, and do the maths with the real number.

Adjustment 3: What hourly work must cover itself

Independent and contract workers usually fund their own health insurance, retirement contributions, unpaid admin time, and both halves of payroll taxes where that applies. A common rule of thumb is that a contractor rate needs to be roughly 25–30% above the equivalent employee rate to land in the same place — treat that as a starting estimate, then price your actual costs.

Comparing two offers fairly

  1. Convert both to the same basis (annual, using realistic hours).
  2. Add the value of paid leave, employer retirement contributions, and insurance on the salaried side.
  3. Subtract self-funded costs on the contract side.
  4. Only then compare the two totals.

Key takeaway

Divide by 2,080 for the headline number — then adjust for unpaid weeks, real hours, and self-funded benefits before you decide which offer actually pays more.
