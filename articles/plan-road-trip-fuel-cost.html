How to Estimate Fuel Cost for a Road Trip Before You Leave

By the Calculate Clearly Editorial Team · Educational guide · Last reviewed October 2026

Fuel is usually the second-biggest cost of a road trip after lodging, and the easiest to estimate badly. A five-minute calculation before you leave prevents the classic surprise at the third fuel stop.

The core formula

Fuel needed = Trip distance ÷ Fuel economy, then Cost = Fuel needed × Price per unit.

Example: 900 miles in a car that truly averages 32 miles per gallon, with fuel at $3.60 a gallon: 900 ÷ 32 ≈ 28.1 gallons, costing about $101.

Use your real fuel economy, not the brochure number

The official rating is measured in controlled conditions. Fully loaded cars, roof boxes, motorway speeds above 70 mph, mountains, and constant air-conditioning all lower it. If you know your car's typical tank-to-tank figure, use that. If not, reduce the official combined rating by about 10% for a loaded highway trip and you will usually land close.

Add the distance you will actually drive

The map's headline distance is only the spine of the trip. Add:

  • Detours and sightseeing loops — often 5–10% extra.
  • Driving at the destination: restaurants, trailheads, visiting friends.
  • A wrong-turn and construction buffer. Planning on exactly the mapped miles almost always underestimates.

A practical approach: calculate the spine, then multiply the distance by 1.1.

Prices change along the route

Fuel prices can differ sharply between regions and between motorway services and town stations. If your route crosses into a cheaper area, planning a main fill there can trim the total — but never let the tank run critically low chasing a saving; the few dollars are not worth a breakdown risk in an unfamiliar place.

A quick pre-trip checklist

  1. Total distance, multiplied by 1.1.
  2. Real-world fuel economy figure.
  3. Average expected price along the route (use the higher area's price for a safe budget).
  4. Divide, multiply, then round up to the nearest $10 for your budget line.

Key takeaway

Honest distance, honest fuel economy, realistic price. With those three, your estimate will land within a few dollars of reality.
